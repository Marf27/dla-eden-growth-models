# 🌌 Diffusion-Limited Aggregation and Eden Growth Model

Simulation and comparison of two stochastic cluster-growth models:

* **Diffusion-Limited Aggregation (DLA)**
* **Eden growth model**

The project investigates how different stochastic growth mechanisms produce distinct cluster morphologies and analyzes their **fractal dimension** and **surface roughness**.

## Overview

Many physical systems exhibit complex structures that emerge from relatively simple stochastic growth processes. Two classical models used to study these phenomena are **Diffusion-Limited Aggregation (DLA)** and the **Eden growth model**.

In this project, both models are implemented on a two-dimensional square lattice and initialized from a single central seed. The resulting clusters are analyzed and compared through their geometric properties.

The simulation focuses on three main aspects:

1. **Cluster morphology** — visualization of the structures generated by each model.
2. **Fractal dimension** — estimation of how the number of occupied sites scales with the cluster radius.
3. **Cluster roughness** — quantification of fluctuations in the cluster boundary.

The stability of the estimated fractal dimension is also investigated as the number of growth events increases.

## Models

### Diffusion-Limited Aggregation

In the DLA model, particles are released at a distance from an existing cluster and perform random walks. A particle becomes permanently attached when it reaches a site adjacent to the cluster.

The growth process can be summarized as:

1. Start with a single occupied site at the center of the lattice.
2. Release a random walker from a circle surrounding the cluster.
3. Let the walker perform a random walk.
4. If the walker reaches a site adjacent to the cluster, it becomes attached.
5. Repeat the process until the desired number of walkers has been released.

This process produces highly branched and irregular structures characteristic of diffusion-limited growth.

### Eden Growth Model

The Eden model represents a different stochastic growth mechanism. Starting from a central seed, occupied sites are selected randomly and one of their neighboring empty sites is occupied.

The process is:

1. Start with a single occupied site.
2. Randomly select an occupied site.
3. Randomly select one of its nearest neighbors.
4. If the neighboring site is empty, occupy it.
5. Repeat until the desired number of growth events has been attempted.

Compared with DLA, Eden clusters tend to be more compact and have smoother boundaries.

## Mathematical Analysis

### Fractal Dimension

The fractal dimension is estimated using the scaling relationship

$$
N(R) \sim R^D,
$$

where:

* $N(R)$ is the number of occupied sites within a radius $R$,
* $R$ is the distance from the cluster center,
* $D$ is the estimated fractal dimension.

Taking logarithms gives

$$
\log N(R) = D \log R + C,
$$

so the fractal dimension can be estimated from the slope of a linear fit in log-log space:

$$
D = \frac{d\log N(R)}{d\log R}.
$$

The code evaluates the number of occupied sites for a range of radii and performs a linear regression in logarithmic coordinates.

### Cluster Roughness

The cluster roughness is defined as the standard deviation of the highest occupied site in each occupied column.

For the set of column heights $h_i$, the roughness is calculated as

$$
W =
\sqrt{
\frac{1}{N}
\sum_{i=1}^{N}
\left(h_i-\langle h\rangle\right)^2
},
$$

where $\langle h\rangle$ is the mean cluster height.

A larger value of $W$ indicates stronger fluctuations in the cluster boundary.

## Simulation Parameters

The main simulation parameters are defined at the beginning of the script:

| Parameter        |    Value | Description                           |
| ---------------- | -------: | ------------------------------------- |
| `GRID_SIZE`      |       50 | Size of the two-dimensional lattice   |
| `INITIAL_RADIUS` |       10 | Initial radius for DLA walker release |
| `MAX_STEPS`      |   10,000 | Maximum steps per DLA random walker   |
| `NUM_WALKERS`    | 20–1,000 | Number of growth events analyzed      |

The simulations are performed on a $50\times50$ lattice.

## Results

The program produces three main outputs.

### 1. Cluster Morphology

The final DLA and Eden clusters are displayed side by side, allowing their different growth patterns to be compared visually.

DLA generally produces a more branched and sparse structure, whereas the Eden model produces a more compact cluster.

### 2. Fractal Dimension

The estimated fractal dimensions of both models are printed for the largest simulated cluster.

The program also evaluates the estimated fractal dimension for increasing numbers of growth events:

$$
N = 20,\ 50,\ 100,\ 200,\ 500,\ 1000.
$$

This allows the convergence and stability of the fractal-dimension estimate to be investigated.

### 3. Cluster Roughness

The final roughness of both clusters is calculated and printed, providing a quantitative measure of their boundary fluctuations.

## Scientific Context

DLA and Eden growth models are simple examples of how **local stochastic rules can generate complex macroscopic structures**.

DLA is particularly relevant to systems involving diffusion-limited transport and aggregation, while the Eden model provides a classical model of stochastic interface growth.

Comparing the two models illustrates how modifying the microscopic growth mechanism can substantially change the resulting morphology and scaling properties.
